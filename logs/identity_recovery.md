## Step 0 — Filer-provided recipient EINs: zero, definitively

`grant_transactions.recipient_ein_raw` exists in the schema and is populated for
**0 of 5,120,094** grant rows. To rule out a parser gap rather than a data gap,
I scanned 3,000 raw filings (42,695 grant groups) and enumerated **every**
element name that appears inside `GrantOrContributionPdDurYrGrp`:

```
RecipientBusinessName, BusinessNameLine1Txt, BusinessNameLine2Txt,
RecipientPersonNm, AddressLine1Txt, AddressLine2Txt, CityNm,
StateAbbreviationCd, ZIPCd, RecipientForeignAddress, CountryCd,
ForeignPostalCd, ProvinceOrStateNm, RecipientFoundationStatusTxt,
RecipientRelationshipTxt, GrantOrContributionPurposeTxt, Amt
```

**There is no EIN field.** The only EIN-shaped strings my scan flagged were
9-digit ZIP+4 codes — a false positive in my own detector, worth noting because
it is the same substring-matching failure family this project keeps hitting.

The empty column is correct behaviour, not a bug. This free win does not exist.

## Step 1 — Alias tranche 2: the proposals are mostly wrong

500 rows / $27.918B parked; 341 carry a proposed EIN. I re-validated every
proposal against the BMF before treating any of it as actionable.

**Only 36.5% of token-containment proposals survive**: the filed name's tokens
are not present in the proposed organisation's BMF name. The failures are gross:

attach a church's group ruling and NTEE code to $168M of donor-advised-fund

produced a *Christian* verdict on a DAF sponsor. That is the ledger-corruption
scenario the gate exists to prevent.

## What I did not do, and why

I stopped before Steps 2–3 rather than proceeding. Two reasons, both honest:

1. **Step 1's finding changes the prior for Steps 2–3.** The parked proposals
   were generated by exactly the kind of token-containment matching Step 3
   proposes to extend. Measuring a 36.5% precision on the existing corpus is
   direct evidence that name-token matching against the BMF is far weaker than
   assumed. Step 3 needs a validation gold set built first, and its threshold
   should be set against this measured baseline — not against the assumption
   that fuzzy matching is safe above some notional cutoff.

2. **The church/GEN work in Step 2 is the strongest remaining lever and
   deserves a clean run**, not a rushed one appended to a pass that has already
   found two false premises. It resolves *and* classifies in one move, which
   also means a bad match writes a wrong tradition — the highest-consequence
   method in the sequence.

## Honest remainder so far
